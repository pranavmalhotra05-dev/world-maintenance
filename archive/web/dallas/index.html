    Hail exposure by ZIP

    This is a hail-swath and building-exposure view, not a lead list. The appraisal-district data (roof material, year built, owner occupancy) and the city permit feed were removed because their licences do not allow commercial reuse. What is left is radar hail at every house-sized building with a street address. "High exposure" means a score of 60 or more. In practice that means 1.25 in+ hail in the last 12 months, 1.5 in+ in the last 24 months, or repeated recent 1 in+ hits. Exposed roof value is the footprint-based roof squares of those homes × $450–650 per square, a rough DFW 2026 shingle price. It is a ceiling, not a forecast, because many of those roofs are already fixed. Click a column to sort.

    Signals

    • NOAA MRMS radar: hail size every 30 min. The watcher re-scores on any 0.75 in+ storm.
    • USGS 3DEP LiDAR (2018–19): the 3D sector scan. One laser echo above ground is a roof; several echoes are tree canopy.
    • Overture Maps buildings (ODbL): every footprint in the county. A "home" is a house-sized footprint (50–600 m²) with one street address.
    • USDOT National Address Database via Overture (public domain): the street address. NAD is not for use as a mailing list.

    Exposure score 0–100

    • Biggest hail in the last 24 months: 60
    • How recent: 25
    • Repeat 1 in+ hail days: 10
    • Roof size: 5
    • Only radar hail and footprint size go in. There is no open data here on roof material, roof age, owner or re-roof permits.

    Limits

    • No re-roof history: many exposed roofs have already been replaced. Verify roofs from the street.
    • Radar hail size is an estimate, capped at 4.5 in.
    • Roof squares are the footprint area × an assumed 1.15 pitch and 5% overhang. They are not measured. The 2018–19 scan is shown for context.
