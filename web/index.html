Why this exists

What a laser scan knows about a neighbourhood's roofs

This page joins a 2023 aircraft LiDAR scan with two kinds of public building records: the assessor roll (year built and use only) and 40 years of building permits (last re-roof, solar). The scan measures every roof's size, pitch, facing and tree cover. The permits date each roof. Together they size the roofing, solar and tree-care work in a neighbourhood, and show where it sits on the map.

Doing it right

Limits and rules

What the data can miss

  • Unpermitted re-roofs look old. Roof age from permits is a lower bound on how recently a roof was replaced.
  • San Francisco files most solar under electrical permits, which aren't loaded yet.
  • The scan is from 2023. Trees grow, and roofs get replaced.

Buildings, not people

  • DataSF Terms of Use §VI: users will not re-identify the data or "contact individuals whose information is represented by the Data".
  • So this page has no owner names, no owner-occupancy (homeowner exemption), no sale dates, no household scores, no contact lists and no export. Only physical facts per building and totals for the area.

How the numbers are made

  • Roof age: years since the last completed roof permit, or "none since 1983" (permit records start then).
  • Roof size: LiDAR roof surface (slope-corrected) in roofing squares (100 ft²).
  • Solar fit: about 30% of the flat or sun-facing area at 210 W/m², capped at 10 kW, × 1,450 kWh per kW per year.
  • Replacement value (totals only): squares × $900–1,400, a rough Bay Area 2026 range.
